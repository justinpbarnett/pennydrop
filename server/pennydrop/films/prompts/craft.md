## Film craft

Narration is the spine of the Film.
The storyboard cuts every shot to a stretch of it, and the people on screen act out what it says as it is said.

### Narration

The viewer hears each line once, at the pace of speech, while watching it acted out.
Write a story told aloud, not a report read on a page.

- Each line is one spoken beat of 30 to 55 words, in plain spoken sentences a smart fifteen year old follows on one hearing. A sentence says one thing, and most sentences are under fifteen words.
- Each line follows from the line before. It opens on what the last line left open, and the lines are joined by cause and turn: so, but, then, because of that. A line that could be moved elsewhere in the Film without anyone noticing is a list item, not a story beat.
- Time only moves forward. Never step back to an earlier moment.
- Follow one person. Name at most five other people in the whole Film, and call everyone else by their role: his brother, a powerful deputy, his secretary. The first time a person is named, say who they are, and after that call them by that one name.
- A number is said only when it is the point of the line, and then rounded the way a person says it: about forty thousand men, three million votes. A line says at most two numbers, counting a year as one. Dates are anchors, not a ledger: give a year when the story moves to a new one, and let the rest be then, that night, a year later.
- Write numbers the way a person says them: "seventeen sixty nine", "sixty five thousand five hundred thirty six", "hex three A five C", "four megabytes". Never write digits or symbols in narration.
- Names are written as they are normally spelled, with accents. The voice reads them through a pronunciation table, and the check lists any name it still mishears.
- Spell the way an American does: harbor, center, color, not harbour, centre, colour. The voice is American and the check compares against what it said.
- No em dashes. Use commas and full stops.
- Every fact comes from the source pack, and the narration states it plainly. Never name a source in the narration: no "according to", "a report records", "a letter that he reproduces", "historians hold". The fact check keeps the sources, and a claim that needs a witness to stand is cut, not attributed.
- The Film's reading of events is the armature, and the cold open declares it once as a way of seeing ("think of each step as a loan"). After that, tell the story inside that reading. Do not repeat a marker such as "here is one way to read it". Mark a toy example as a toy where it starts.
- A quotation of a person is only ever one that the source pack traces to their own work, their correspondence or a contemporary memoir. Never quote anyone from a popular saying. Quote at most three times in the Film, each quotation short, and only where the words themselves are the event.
- The Thread, the comparison with today, is spoken in the cold open, the payoff and the close, and where it breaks, once and plainly. The middle of the Film tells the story and does not stop for asides about managers or modern life.
- Never talk about the Film itself ("this film shows"). The close asks one question the viewer can carry away.
