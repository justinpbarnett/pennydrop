## The armature and the spine

A Film has one armature.
The armature is one sentence that holds the whole Film up.
It must pass all four tests.

1. It is a claim, a full sentence that could be argued with. A topic ("Napoleon's leadership lessons") is not a claim.
2. It names no person and no event, so it carries to any case of the same kind. The Film proves it with its people and events, and the armature itself never mentions them.
3. It holds a tension, two forces pulling against each other, so the Film has something to dramatize.
4. It can be tested tactic by tactic against the evidence, so it can fail. A claim that cannot be wrong is not an armature.

Every scene serves the armature, and the Film dramatizes it instead of stating it.
Tell them what you will tell them (the cold open is the promise), tell them (the scenes), tell them what you told them (the payoff restates the armature in fresh words).
Economy and precision: a scene that does not serve the armature is cut, however good it is.

The story spine has eight sentences, in this order and starting with these words.

1. Once upon a time ...
2. Every day ...
3. But one day ...
4. Because of that ...
5. Because of that ...
6. Because of that ...
7. Until finally ...
8. And ever since then ...

The cold open is the promise and serves sentence 1.
The twist is "but one day" and breaks the everyday pattern.
The payoff restates the armature and serves sentence 7.
The closing open questions come from "ever since then" and serve sentence 8.
