# Pennydrop Strategy

## Vision

<!-- vision: author-approved 2026-11-23 -->

Pennydrop exists so that a person can ask about anything, by voice or text, and watch the answer happen until the penny drops.
It serves two learners: an adult studying computer science at graduate level, and a young child who cannot read yet.
It turns an Ask into a short narrated Lesson that proves one point on a drawn stage, and a Topic worth more into a Film watched before its Lessons.
It owns exactly one thing: the path from a question to a Rule the learner can use on a case they have never seen.

### Code computes, the model intends

Every number, trace step, trajectory and answer on stage comes from running code: a tracer, a simulation or a math engine.
The model picks Labs and operations and writes the words; it never writes coordinates, SVG or drawing code.
A sentence the computed facts disprove is cut from the Lesson, even when that leaves the Step shorter.
The writer types an answer itself only when no Lab can run the case.
Where no code can compute the answer, as in history, a Lab still shows the evidence on stage (a map, a timeline, a source), and a second model family checks every claim before it plays.
An Ask for an opinion gets no Lesson, because an opinion holds no Rule to practice.
The player draws each frame from the timeline value, never the clock, so any moment can be sought and draws the same every time.

### Every Lesson proves one point

A Lesson states its point as one full sentence claim before Step 1, and a topic label is not a point.
Step 1 asks the question the Lesson answers, in the learner's world.
The Steps follow a spine: the normal case, the break, a chain of causes, the reveal, and the point said again.
The predict Challenge sits where the learner can add up what they saw and reach the answer before the reveal.
The stage shows the point happening; narration that only states it is not enough.
A Step that does not move the point is cut, however true.
Every Ask gets a full Lesson; there is no quick answer mode that skips the spine or Practice.
Story quality outranks speed: speed is won inside the full Lesson, and a faster path that breaks the story is removed.
Quality is raised in code (plans, computed answers, checks and repairs) rather than by moving the writer to the costliest model; the strongest model judges Lessons instead.

### Understanding means using the Rule

Each Lesson teaches one Rule, with the condition it holds under.
Practice follows the story in three Forms: say the Rule with a because, judge a near miss, and predict a fresh case.
Review is keyed by Rule, and every review computes a new case, so recalling an old answer never passes for skill.
Progress counts Rules held, never Lessons watched.
A reward, such as a star, marks a Rule held; nothing rewards a streak, a visit or time spent.

### Built for the learner in front of it

A Profile sets how a Lesson is pitched and drawn, and an adult's work never lands on a child's Profile.
For a child who cannot read, nothing on screen needs reading: voice in and out, pictures, big tap targets, and every Challenge something a finger can answer.
A thing keeps its real color in every Theme, so a blue sky stays blue.
Kid Lessons pass a content check, and the parent can read the lesson log.
Any Lab can be opened for free play, and asking what just happened turns the current Scene into an Ask.
A learner's own lecture slides and readings may ground a Lesson's terms and examples.
Coursework Lessons explain concepts on toy examples and never read or write a graded assignment or its spec.

### The library grows, the stack stays owned

Each Lab owns its layout and speaks its system's operations, and every Theme draws every Lab.
When no Lab fits, a Lesson falls back to primitives, and the Mint builds a Lab that must render and pass a judge.
A minted Lab joins the repo only after a review reads its code against the system it models, because a judge that sees frames cannot tell whether the Lab models its system right.
Pennydrop runs its own AI through one gateway, on subscriptions first and API keys as backup, and depends on no outside agent platform.
Voice providers, Themes and model routes swap by config, and committed audio and art are generated, open or CC0.
A Lesson costs cents and a Film costs a few dollars.
Learner data (Profiles, answers, lesson logs, uploaded course material) stays in the local store and out of git.

### Scope

Pennydrop is not a chatbot that answers in text, not a video generator, not a flashcard deck, not a language course, not a course marketplace and not a homework solver.
It is not a hosted service: its model routes run on its owner's own subscriptions, which cannot serve other people, so anyone else runs their own copy.
A Film is its own work, staged as Blender dioramas where people act out the events and fact checked by a second model family, never a recorded Lesson.
Films are made for Pennydrop's learners; publishing them to an outside audience belongs to a separate project.

A change aligns when it helps a learner reach and hold a Rule faster or more surely, keeps every shown fact computed or checked, and keeps each Lesson on its one point.
A change should be resisted when it lets the model draw or state a fact code could compute, skips the story or Practice for speed, rewards watching or streaks, promotes a minted Lab on its frames alone, serves people beyond its own learners, or moves learner data into the repo.
